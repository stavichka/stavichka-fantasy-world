Under the hood
How it works

Where the data comes from and how every number is built.

Photo: Max Winkler / Unsplash

What updates, and when

A scheduled task runs every morning (UK time) through the signed-in browser in your Claude desktop app. Your computer needs to be on with the app open; if it isn't, that day's update is skipped and the page keeps the last data. Each run:

  • Pulls four injury reports: ESPN (with its return dates), CBS Sports, Covers and Basketball-Reference. Each gives a return estimate (Covers and Basketball-Reference from timelines in their news notes, such as "re-evaluated in 3-4 weeks"), and the middle estimate is used. Hover an injury tag, or open a player, to see every source.
  • Pulls live ESPN data: every roster (so waiver adds, drops, trades and IR moves are reflected), matchup results, standings, injury status, this season's stats, ESPN's projections and player ages.
  • Pulls projections and rankings from a wide set of expert and statistical sources and runs them through the Stavichka Projection Matrix.
  • Rebuilds everything below and republishes this page's data. The "Updated" stamp at the top shows when that last happened.

Stavichka Projection Matrix

  • Every number labelled "Proj FP/G" is the Stavichka Projection Matrix, in your league's exact scoring (read from ESPN each run, so a scoring change is picked up automatically).
  • Own model: last season and the season before, each aged forward, plus a calibrated outside projection. Once games start, this season's production is mixed in; 25 games played counts as much as the whole prior.
  • Outside inputs: season-long projections, next-game simulations, expert rankings and team-preview notes from many sources, each converted to your scoring and weighted by how reliable it is. Inputs that only cover part of the league, or that still show last season, count less or are left out automatically.
  • Short samples: a player with fewer than 45 games in a season is pulled toward a typical part-time player in proportion to the games missing, so 13 strong games don't count as a full season.
  • Rest of season (ROS): once games start, "ROS FP/G" is what the Matrix expects per game from today on, and "This season" shows what actually happened. ROS games are his NBA team's remaining games, minus games he's expected to miss injured, times how often he plays (his recent seasons, with this season counting more as games pile up). ROS points = ROS games × ROS FP/G. Before the season, the same columns cover the whole season.
  • Rookies with no projection anywhere get an estimate from their draft slot, shown as "est.", until the Matrix has real inputs for them.

Projection accuracy

This week, trends, playoffs and trade ideas

  • This week: your matchup with points so far (from ESPN), games used against the cap, the rest of the week planned with your best lineup for both teams, and the win chance. Light days (7 or fewer NBA games) are flagged, with your open slots and the best free agents playing that day.
  • Trends: fantasy points per game over the last 7, 15 and 30 days, and minutes, against the ROS projection. Sell high: 20%+ above his projection over 15 days without more minutes. Buy low: 15%+ below it with his minutes holding. Bigger role: 4+ more minutes a game lately. Needs at least 4 games in the last 15 days.
  • Playoff schedule: games in your fantasy playoff weeks for every NBA team and your players, with light-day games and back-to-backs. Trades also show the change in playoff-week games for each side's best 9.
  • Trade ideas: checks every one-for-one and two-for-one deal with each team, keeps those that help both sides by what each is aiming for, and re-scores the best with the season simulation.

Dynasty value

The Matrix rolls each projection forward five years with an aging curve, scores it above a replacement level of the 190th-best player with later seasons discounted, and blends that with expert dynasty rankings. The result is on a 0–100 scale.

Season and playoffs

12,000 simulated seasons on the real schedule. Completed weeks use actual results. The current week keeps the points already scored and simulates the rest. Each week the best available 9 start, with availability based on projected games and injuries: each injured player is unavailable until his estimated return date (the middle of the four injury reports' estimates), counted week by week. While a player is out, 60% of his projected production is shared among his healthy NBA teammates in the rotation, more to those who play his position, and capped at +15% for any one player; the green "+" next to a projection shows that bump. Players flagged out by ESPN with no report fall back to about 2 weeks (4 on IR). Playoffs are simulated in your league's bracket format.

Your win-now / future setting

One 1–10 setting drives keepers, the draft plan, free-agent suggestions and the trade score. 1 means winning this season above all, 10 means long-term value above all. Players are scored on a blend of their projection rank and dynasty rank, weighted by that setting. Each viewer's setting is saved in their own browser.

Draft plan and free agents

Until the 11 October draft, the My team tab shows a draft plan: the draft is simulated pick by pick. Every team keeps its best 9, the other teams take the best player left on a balanced board, and you take the best player left by your setting, or by the target you set for that pick (its own win-now/future level, a position, or rookie / 2nd-year / veteran). If nobody matching a pick's target is likely to be left, the best available player is shown instead. From 12 October it shows the best available free agents instead, each paired with the roster spot it would best replace.

Weekly lineup planner

Your league is head to head with daily lineups (9 starters: PG, SG, SF, PF, C, C, G, F, UTIL) and a cap of 34 games started per 7-day week (29 in the 6-day opening week, 68 in the 14-day All-Star week). For each week the planner lists every game your players have on ESPN's NBA schedule, drops games before an injured player's estimated return, then fills starts from the highest projection down, skipping any game that would break a day's lineup or go over the cap. With equal value for each of a player's games, this greedy fill gives the best possible total. The same weekly totals now set each team's expected score in the season simulation.

Today's waiver moves

Each morning, for your current matchup: every add (from the 60 best free agents with a game left this week) paired with each of your four least valuable players by your win-now / future setting is run through the lineup planner for the rest of the week, using the games cap you have left. The extra points are turned into a change in your chance of beating this week's opponent, using both teams' projected weeks and their usual week-to-week swings. The six moves that add the most win probability are shown, each with the dynasty value you gain or give up.

Trade machine

  • This season: each team's weekly scoring is scaled by its change in projected starting lineup (best legal PG, SG, SF, PF, C, C, G, F, UTIL). The rest of the season and the playoffs are then re-simulated 4,000 times, using the same random draws before and after so the differences reflect the trade rather than chance.
  • Keeper value: the sum of a team's 9 most valuable assets on the 0–100 dynasty scale, counting draft picks alongside players.
  • Draft picks: each pick is valued by the typical value of a player taken at that slot. That curve is fitted to this year's draft pool: about 65 for the 1st live pick, 13 for the 9th, 4 for the 30th, 2 for the 60th.
    • October 2026 picks use the actual pick numbers.
    • 2027 and 2028 picks assume reverse-standings snake drafts. They are valued from each team's simulated finish (2028 is regressed halfway to an average finish) and discounted for time (85% and 72%).
    • A trade that makes a team better automatically lowers the value of its own future picks.
  • Trade score: a rough summary that converts 1 projected win to 10 keeper-value points (about one starter-tier player), weighted by each side's own 1–10 aim (1 counts only projected wins, 10 only keeper value). Your team uses your saved setting; for other teams the aim starts as an estimate from their playoff and title odds and the age of their best 9 players, and you can move it.
  • Balancer: when one side loses out, it tries every single change (the side gaining more adds one of its players or picks, or the other side takes one back), screens each with a quick re-simulation, and re-scores the best with the full simulation. If no single change is enough it tries pairs. It ranks changes by how far the losing side still falls short and how far apart the two scores are.
  • Assumption: every team still owns its own future picks. ESPN doesn't track pick trades, so tell me if picks have moved and I'll update the ownership table.

Limits

Not modelled: the NBA's weekly game counts per team, streaming pickups, and exact injury return dates. Before the keeper deadline and draft, rosters are the current 16-man rosters, so the standings will shift once keepers and the draft are done.
